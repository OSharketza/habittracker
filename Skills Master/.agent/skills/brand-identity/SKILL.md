# Maintaining Brand Identity

Keep the project's personality consistent and recognizable across all touchpoints.

## When to use this skill
- Designing new UI components or pages.
- Writing user-facing documentation or notifications.
- Brainstorming marketing or landing page copy.
- Reviewing feature names and nomenclature.

## Workflow

1.  **Define Brand Pillars**: Identify 3-5 keywords that describe the brand (e.g., "Professional," "Playful," "Minimalist").
2.  **Visual Audit**: Cross-reference new designs with the existing color palette, typography, and spacing system.
3.  **Voice & Tone Check**: Ensure text is consistent with the brand's personality (e.g., "Helpful coach" vs. "Academic authority").
4.  **Logo & Asset Usage**: Verify that official assets are used according to spacing and placement rules.
5.  **Review Consistency**: Check if the "personality" of the new feature matches the rest of the application.

## Core Pillars (Generic Template)

### 1. Visual Identity
- **Color Palette**: Primary, secondary, and accent usage.
- **Typography**: Header vs. Body font pairings.
- **Iconography**: Style (e.g., Solid vs. Outline, Rounded vs. Sharp).

### 2. Voice and Tone
- **Diction**: Choice of words (e.g., "Log in" vs. "Sign in," "Awesome" vs. "Confirmed").
- **Communication Style**: Direct, empathetic, witty, or formal.

### 3. User Experience Personality
- **Transitions**: Smooth and subtle vs. fast and snappy.
- **Feedback**: How the app "talks" back on success or failure.

## Best Practices
- **Style Over Trends**: Follow the established brand even if a new design trend emerges.
- **Consistency is Trust**: Small variations in color or tone can make the app feel "broken" to a user.
- **Document Decisions**: Use the `Brand Guide` resource to store project-specific values.
